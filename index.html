GROUND SOLAR + MINI HYDRO

Daytime curtailment watch.

Find dates and sample windows with unusual output reductions.

Flags are not confirmed curtailment. National aggregate readings can flag suspicious dates, but cannot identify individual curtailed plants or establish lost energy.

Adjust the screening rules

A step screen looks for an abrupt reduction from the median of the previous three readings, followed by a sustained lower run. A historical screen looks for readings below a seven-calendar-day reference, with at least four complete preceding days. Both apply an absolute floor: 5 MW for ground solar and 10 MW for mini hydro. Historical screening also uses a three-scaled-MAD tolerance. These are adjustable investigation heuristics, not validated curtailment detection thresholds. Solar dawn and dusk, clouds, water changes and metering issues can still trigger flags.

How to confirm a curtailment candidate

Check the operator's NSO/DCC instruction, dispatch or inverter active-power setpoint logs, and plant availability for the same times. Solar needs irradiance or other available-power evidence; mini hydro needs flow, head and machine-availability records. The public aggregate alone cannot establish these. This screen keeps rooftop solar and estimated mini hydro separate and does not fill missing series with broad group totals.

A sampled span is the time between flagged labels, not the duration of a confirmed restriction. A difference from recent output is not available power, curtailed MW or lost MWh. Repeated restrictions in the reference week can mask later restrictions. No flags does not prove no curtailment.

SCREENING RULE

Unusual downward changes

Consecutive 15-minute source observations falling by at least 100 MW and 30% are flagged. Aggregated series can contain multiple plants.

A flag is not a confirmed trip, outage or cause. Gaps, normal dispatch and measurement effects can produce changes. Check official records.
